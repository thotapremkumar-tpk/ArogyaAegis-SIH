Notes

  • The J3 header only holds the board up. Its G pins are ground through the DevKit.
  • IO3 is a strapping pin, but a stock DevKit never samples it, so an analog input there is safe.
  • I²C addresses don't clash: OLED 0x3C, MPU-6050 0x68, MAX30102 0x57 (optional, on J5). Leave the MPU's AD0 pin open; the GY-521 board pulls it low.
  • The DRV8833 is not used. Each motor gets the 2N2222 stage that worked in Demo 1, plus a 10 kΩ base pull-down so the motors stay off while the ESP32 boots.

Rules for this power chain

  • Set the MT3608 to 5.0 V with the multimeter before it touches the DevKit. Many arrive set far higher.
  • Charge with the switch off. The TP4056 shouldn't feed a load while it charges.
  • Plug USB into the DevKit only with the switch off. Espressif treats USB and the 5V pin as separate power options, so use one at a time.
  • Motors run straight from the battery rail through the transistors, so their current never passes the ESP32's regulator.

Why two grounds

Power GND (brown on the drawing) carries motor and battery current on row 22. Signal GND (black) serves the ESP32 and sensors on row 19. They meet at one point: the MT3608's ground pad, which is wired to J1-22. Motor current never flows through the ECG's ground, which keeps buzzes out of the trace.

7 · Phase 3

Soldering the perfboard

Tools and settings

  • Iron at 350–380 °C if it's adjustable; your 22 SWG solder is lead-free and needs the heat. A fixed iron needs 3–5 minutes to warm up.
  • Tin the tip and keep it shiny: wipe, then add a touch of solder.
  • Flux paste: a thin smear on the pads only.
  • Open window, eye protection, iron always back in its stand.
  • Battery unplugged whenever the iron is on.

A good joint

  1. Touch the tip to the pad and the pin together for about a second.
  2. Feed solder into the joint on the far side of the pin, not onto the tip.
  3. Stop once it flows into a small cone; 1–2 mm of wire is enough for a header pin.
  4. Take the solder away, then the iron. The whole joint takes under 3 seconds.
  5. Hold still for 2 seconds. Lead-free joints look satin, not mirror-bright.

A ball sitting on the pad means the pad was too cold; reheat with flux. A bridge between pads comes off by dragging a fluxed tip between them.

Perfboard techniques

  • Rails and bare links (dashed): tinned wire or resistor-leg offcuts laid flat along the holes on the bottom, soldered at every hole. Pull the wire to straighten it first.
  • Insulated wires (solid): strip 3 mm, push through the hole next to the target pad from the top, bend onto the pad underneath, solder. Keep them flat.
  • Upright resistors (R1–R4, R6, R7): bend one lead back along the body so both leads drop into neighbouring holes.
  • JST sockets: use the XH sockets (2.5 mm pitch) on the board. PH sockets (2.0 mm) don't line up with 2.54 mm holes. If a kit pair has wire on both halves, solder one pigtail into the board and hot-glue it.

Build order

  1. Dry-fit every module and mark the header holes with a marker. Cut the board down if it's bigger than your enclosure.
  2. Plug two 1 × 22 female strips onto the DevKit, stand it on rows 23 and 32, tack the four corner pins, lift the DevKit off, solder the rest.
  3. Remaining headers and sockets: AD8232, OLED, MPU, P1, J5–J10.
  4. Rails: 3.3 V (row 15, left), signal GND (row 19), battery (row 15, right), power GND (row 22, right).
  5. Low parts: diodes, resistors, C3, transistors, then C1 and C2. Copy the transistor's E-B-C order from your working breadboard: a PN2222A reads E-B-C with the flat face toward you, a P2N2222A reads C-B-E.
  6. Bare links, then insulated wires group by group, beeping and ticking each one.
  7. MT3608, already set to 5.0 V: foam-tape it down and wire its four pads.
  8. Scrub off flux with alcohol and a toothbrush, then check every joint through a zoomed phone camera.

First power-on

  1. No modules plugged in. Beep 5V–GND, 3V3–GND and battery+–GND: none may beep steadily. A short chirp is the capacitors charging.
  2. Battery in, switch on: 5.0 V at C4 R23 and 3.0–4.2 V on the battery rail. Switch off.
  3. DevKit only, USB unplugged. Switch on: it boots and the 3.3 V bus reads 3.25–3.35 V.
  4. Add modules one at a time, rerunning the matching test (B3–B8) after each.
  5. Finish with B9, with the board in the pouch.

Safety

  • Check the LiPo plug polarity before every new connection; plug colours aren't standard.
  • Never short or puncture the LiPo. Charge on a non-flammable surface, not unattended. Stop using it if it swells or gets hot.
  • Electrodes on a person only while running from the battery, never while charging or plugged into a laptop on its charger.
  • ArogyaAegis is a research prototype, not a medical device.

Mounting it in a vest

Cable rules

  • Silicone stranded wire (24–26 AWG) for every lead off the board. Solid-core wire snaps after repeated bending, so it stays on the perfboard.
  • A JST plug at the pouch end of every lead, labelled M1, M2, FLEX, GSR, so the pouch unplugs in seconds.
  • Strain relief at both ends: heat-shrink over each solder joint, then stitch or tie the wire to the fabric 2–3 cm away, so a tug pulls on the fabric and not the joint.
  • Slack across joints: leave a 3–5 cm loop at the shoulder, waist and wrist.
  • Hold leads in sewn channels (bias tape) or elastic loops every 8–10 cm. Adhesive alone won't stay on stretchy fabric.
  • Twist each motor's two wires together and keep them on the back, at least 10 cm from the ECG lead.

Building the vest

  1. Put the vest on and mark the pad spots, band height, pouch and motor spots with tailor's chalk. Try everything with safety pins before sewing.
  2. Sew the pouch (or two strips of sew-on Velcro) on the left side, and cut a 1 cm slit beside it for leads to pass inside.
  3. Sew two small pockets on the upper back for the motors, open at the top.
  4. Make the breathing band: 3–4 cm elastic, a little shorter than your chest, Velcro closure, flex sleeve sewn at the front.
  5. Sew cable channels down the front (ECG), along the band (flex), down the back (motors) and down the left side (GSR).
  6. Where you use your self-adhesive Velcro, add a stitch at each corner; the adhesive peels off stretchy fabric.
  7. Make the leads: silicone wire, XH plug at the board end, heat-shrink at both ends.
  8. Fit the enclosure: slots for the TP4056's USB-C and the switch, a window for the OLED, battery taped in clear of sharp edges.
  9. Rerun test B9 while walking, sitting and bending, and re-route any lead that pulls.

Putting it on

  1. Switch off. Wipe the three pad spots with an alcohol swab and let them dry.
  2. Stick on fresh gel pads and snap the leads by the letters on the clips.
  3. Put the vest on; feed the ECG lead down inside and out through the slit.
  4. Fasten the band snug enough to stay put while two fingers still slide under it.
  5. Put on the finger straps and wristband.
  6. Plug in every lead and the ECG jack, switch on, and wait for "Paired" on the dashboards.
  7. Check BPM, the breathing wave and the GSR value, then type t to test the motors.

To take it off: switch off, unplug the leads, lift the pouch out, peel the pads.

Comfort, washing and safety

  • No hard part over a bone. Round off the enclosure corners and keep the pouch on the soft flank.
  • The battery stays inside the enclosure, never against skin. After 30 minutes the pouch should not feel warm.
  • Electrodes only while running from the battery: never while charging, never while USB goes to a laptop on its charger.
  • Before washing, remove the pouch and unplug every lead. Wipe the flex sensor and GSR straps; never soak them. Hand-wash the vest cold and air-dry it.
  • Gel pads are single use.
  • Testing on other people needs your department's approval and their consent, as the project report sets out.
